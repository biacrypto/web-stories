How Dollar-Cost Averaging Works

DCA is the simplest investing strategy there is: invest the same dollar amount on a fixed schedule — say $100 on the 1st of every month — no matter what the price is doing. When the price drops, your $100 buys more coins. When it rises, it buys fewer. Over time this smooths out the wild swings crypto is famous for.

The chart in the tool above tells the story better than words: the gold line is your DCA portfolio growing month by month, and the dashed blue line is what would have happened if you'd put everything in on day one. DCA vs Lump Sum: Which Wins?

There's no universal winner — it depends on the market:

  • Lump sum usually wins in steady uptrends. Money invested earlier has more time to grow.
  • DCA usually wins in crashes and choppy markets. Fixed buys automatically load up at the bottom.
  • DCA wins psychologically, always. No regret from "buying the top," no panic decisions — the schedule decides for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is DCA (dollar-cost averaging)?

Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of price. You automatically buy more when prices are low and less when they're high.

Does DCA beat lump-sum investing?

Sometimes. Lump sum wins in rising markets; DCA wins in falling or sideways markets. The tool above backtests both so you can compare for any coin and period.

Are these real historical prices?

No — the tool uses illustrative monthly closes (updated Oct 2026) for educational comparison, not live market data. Always check live prices on an exchange before investing.

How much should I invest monthly?

Only what you can afford to lose. A common beginner rule: a small fixed % of income that you won't miss if it drops 50%. This tool is for learning — not financial advice.
